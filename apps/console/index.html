A full TinCAN node. It transmits, acknowledges, arbitrates and confines errors. Open it on two devices in the same room, set them to the same band and timing profile, and they form a bus. Open it on one device and it will transmit into an empty room, go error-passive, and tell you nobody is listening — which is exactly what §8.3 says should happen. If two devices will not talk, run the self-test on each first.

When two devices will not talk
  • The self-test fails — this browser cannot run a node; try another browser.
  • The microphone check says a device does not hear itself — its speaker is muted or too quiet, or echo cancellation is on. Nothing else will work until it does.
  • "no node acknowledged" on every frame — the other device is not joined, is muted, is on a different band or bit timing, or cannot hear this one. Watch its Signal panel while this one sends.
  • Bit errors on this node's own frames — it hears itself too late or through too much echo. Try T10, which tolerates more of both; the microphone check gives the figure.
  • The carrier pill never goes out, and the queue never empties — the room is louder in the TinCAN band than the detector expects, or another sound source is on these frequencies.
  • Collisions whenever both devices have something to send — each hears itself too loudly to hear the other's dominant bits (spec §4.6). Move the devices closer together, or wait for one to finish.
  • "delivered" here but nothing arrives there — a false acknowledgement: in a reverberant room a node can mistake its own echo for one. Compare counters on both devices, and use a deader room.
