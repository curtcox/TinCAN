Bands

Every TinCAN band is two tones, recessive and dominant, with a guard tone either side to measure the noise (Part 1 §4.2). The band decides who can hear the bus, how far it carries and what it competes with. It barely decides how fast the bus runs: the bit-timing profile does, at the same rate on every band. The one exception is band E, whose tones sit 250 Hz apart instead of 1250 Hz so as to fit below 1 kHz: telling them apart takes five times longer, so it runs T10 and slower only.

How loud each band seems

For a tone reaching the listener at dB SPL, about the level of conversation across a table. Loudness is the ISO 226 loudness level of the band's louder tone, in phon: equal phon sound equally loud. ISO 226 stops at 12.5 kHz, so for band B the table reports who can hear it at all instead.

Hear them on this device

silent

Each button plays two seconds of a band's tones, alternating bit by bit at T25 speed, at the Console's transmit level. They are the same digital level; what reaches you also depends on the speaker. Turn the volume down first.

Measure them on this device

not run

Plays each band's two tones in turn, about six seconds in all, and records what this device's own microphone hears: whether its speaker and microphone can carry the band at all, and how far above the room's noise. A band its own microphone barely hears will not carry far.

It also compares the two tones. In arbitration one node's dominant tone has to hold its own against another's recessive tone, which it can do arriving up to dB weaker (Part 1 §4.6): in open air, a contender up to times as far away. A speaker and microphone that reproduce the dominant tone weaker than the recessive spend that margin before distance does, on every path between devices like this one. The page warns when what is left allows less than a :1 distance ratio. Below 1 kHz the balance also depends on where the device sits in the room, so measure where it will be used.

How far each band carries

Air absorbs high frequencies faster (ISO 9613-1, 20 °C, 50 % relative humidity), on top of the 6 dB lost each time the distance doubles, which is the same for every band. Furnishings, people and small speakers' narrow beams favour low frequencies further.

How much each band can carry

A profile sets the bit rate, the same on every band that can run it. The bit time has to cover every node's audio delay and the distance between them, because each bit is arbitrated (Part 1 §5.6), so TinCAN uses a sliver of what each band's slot could carry. What a band adds is a channel of its own: two nodes that can do better than the main bus can open a side link on another band (Part 2 §8).
